Pricing the work

Should a fractional CTO take a success fee tied to your raise?

Aug 20265 min readby The founder of Fraction

The pitch sounds aligned: "Lower my monthly rate, and pay me a success fee when you close the round." Or a bonus if you hit a milestone, or a slice of the outcome if the technical diligence goes clean. On the surface it feels like the fractional CTO putting skin in the game, betting on your success instead of just billing you. Should you take the deal?

The direct answer: be careful. A success fee tied to fundraising sounds like alignment but usually creates the wrong incentive, and in some places it raises legal questions you do not want. A success fee tied to a concrete technical deliverable can work. The distinction is what the fee rewards: an outcome the fractional CTO controls, or one they do not.

Why a raise-linked success fee is the wrong target

The problem with a bonus for closing your round is that your fractional CTO does not control whether you raise, and a lot of what does control it, they should be advising you against.

A fractional CTO's job in a fundraise is to tell investors the truth about your technical reality: what is solid, what is shaky, what the real risks are. That honesty is the entire value of having them in the room during technical due diligence. The moment their payout depends on the round closing, you have quietly given them a reason to smooth over the shaky parts, to present the architecture as more mature than it is, to help the deal happen rather than help you understand it. You have taken the one person whose job is unvarnished technical truth and paid them to be an optimist.

There is a legal wrinkle too. In the United States, taking a fee that is contingent on the success of a securities transaction can look like acting as an unregistered broker-dealer, which is regulated. Advisors and their lawyers tend to steer well clear of anything that smells like a commission on a raise for exactly this reason. You do not want your technical advisor's compensation to become a securities-law question in the middle of your round. This is squarely a money-adjacent decision, so treat the accuracy bar as high and get real advice before structuring anything creative.

And the alignment it promises is mostly illusory. The thing you actually want from a fractional CTO is not enthusiasm about your round. It is judgment you can trust precisely because it is not bent by what they stand to gain. A modest, predictable retainer keeps that judgment clean. That is the same reason you are paying for judgment rather than for hours or for outcomes they cannot guarantee.

When an outcome-based fee can actually work

None of this means every performance-based structure is bad. It means the fee has to reward something the fractional CTO genuinely controls and can be measured cleanly. A few structures hold up:

A fee tied to a defined technical deliverable. If the engagement is scoped around shipping a specific thing, migrating off a broken platform, getting SOC 2 controls in place, passing a security review from your biggest customer, then a bonus on delivering that, on time and to an agreed standard, is fair. The fractional CTO controls the work and the finish line is objective.

A retainer with a completion bonus, not a discount-plus-upside. The healthiest version keeps a real retainer, at a rate that stands on its own, and adds a modest bonus for a clearly defined result. Where it goes wrong is when the fractional CTO cuts their base rate deeply in exchange for a big contingent payout. Now they are underpaid unless the bet pays off, which pressures every judgment call toward whatever triggers the payout.

Equity, handled as its own decision. If you want real long-term alignment, equity is the honest instrument for it, and it comes with its own tradeoffs worth thinking through separately. Cash-versus-equity for a fractional CTO is a distinct conversation from a one-off success fee, and it deserves to be treated as one. Fraction has a full breakdown of the equity versus cash tradeoff if that is the alignment you are really after.

The test for any performance fee is one question: does this payment reward the fractional CTO for doing their job well, or for an outcome that tempts them to do their job badly? A delivery bonus rewards good work. A raise-closing bonus rewards a closed raise, including the ones that should not have closed.

How to think about the offer

If a fractional CTO proposes a success fee, do not reject the instinct behind it, which is usually a genuine wish to be aligned with you. Redirect it. Say some version of: "I like that you want skin in the game. Let us tie a bonus to shipping the migration on time, not to the round closing, so your incentive and my technical truth stay on the same side." A good operator will understand immediately, because a good operator already knows a raise-contingent fee compromises the advice you are paying them for.

If instead they push hard for a chunk of the round, treat it as a signal. It may mean they are more interested in the transaction than in your technical reality, which is the opposite of what a fractional CTO is for. The people worth hiring want their compensation clean enough that you never have to wonder whether their advice is bent. When you are structuring an engagement and someone proposes creative comp, it is worth a call to pressure-test it before you sign. You can book a call to talk through what actually aligns.

FAQ

Should I pay my fractional CTO a success fee for closing my funding round?

Generally no. Your fractional CTO does not control whether you raise, and a raise-contingent fee gives them a reason to overstate your technical maturity to investors, which undermines the honest diligence you are paying for. In the US it can also raise unregistered broker-dealer questions. Keep fundraising advice on a clean retainer.

Is any performance-based fee for a fractional CTO acceptable?

Yes, when it rewards something they control and can be measured objectively, like shipping a defined migration on time or getting specific security controls in place. Keep the base retainer at a fair standalone rate and add a modest completion bonus, rather than deeply discounting the rate for a large contingent payout.

What is the difference between a success fee and equity?

A success fee is a one-off payment tied to a specific outcome. Equity is ongoing ownership that aligns the fractional CTO with your long-term value. If you want durable alignment, equity is the more honest instrument, and it should be evaluated as its own decision rather than bolted on as a raise bonus.

How do I respond if a fractional CTO asks for a cut of the raise?

Redirect the incentive toward a technical deliverable they control, and treat a hard push for round-contingent pay as a signal about their priorities. The advisors worth hiring want their compensation clean enough that you never question whether their technical advice is bent by their payout.
