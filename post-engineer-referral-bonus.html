Should your startup pay engineers a referral bonus?

Nov 20265 min readby The founder of Fraction

For a startup with fewer than ten engineers, a referral bonus is worth having, but it is not what produces referrals. Set a clear, modest amount for engineering hires, pay it in two parts tied to the new hire staying, run it through payroll as ordinary taxable pay, and decide in advance who is not eligible. The thing that actually drives referrals is that your team believes the company is a good place to bring a friend.

I get asked about this once a company has two or three engineers and the founder realises that their own network is tapped out. The team's networks are the next obvious channel, and a cash bonus feels like the way to unlock them. It helps at the margin. It does not substitute for the harder parts.

Why referrals matter so much early

For a first or second senior hire, warm introductions usually beat job boards by a wide margin on quality, and they shorten the process because there is already some trust. I covered the sourcing side in where to find your first senior engineer.

A referral from an engineer on your team is even better than one from an advisor, because the engineer has actually worked with the person and knows what the job involves. The catch is that a good engineer will only refer someone they respect if they are happy themselves. Nobody puts a former colleague into a company they are thinking of leaving.

How much to pay

Published figures for engineering referral bonuses mostly come from HR software vendors and vary widely. Ranges of roughly $1,000 to $10,000 are commonly quoted, with a few thousand dollars as a typical middle; Patriot Software's guide to referral payouts is one example of how those numbers are presented. Treat them as direction, not benchmarks.

My rule of thumb for a seed-stage company: pick a single number for engineering hires that is meaningful but not distorting, somewhere in the low thousands of dollars, and compare it to what you would otherwise pay. A contingency recruiter commonly charges a percentage of first-year salary, which for a senior engineer is far more than any reasonable referral bonus. That is the comparison that makes the budget easy to justify.

Do not tier it by seniority at the start. One number is easy to explain and avoids the awkward conversation about why referring a staff engineer pays three times more than referring a mid-level one when both took the same effort.

How to structure it

Pay in two parts

Half when the new hire starts and half after they have stayed for a set period, commonly 90 days or six months. This rewards referring people who actually work out, and it avoids any need to claw money back if someone leaves early.

Write down who is eligible

Decide before the first payout, not during it. Common exclusions at a startup:

  • The hiring manager for the role, and anyone on the interview loop whose job already includes hiring.
  • Founders and executives.
  • Referrals of former employees, unless you decide rehires count. I have views on rehiring an engineer who left that apply here.
  • Candidates already in your pipeline before the referral arrived.

Write this on one page and share it with the team. Ambiguity turns a goodwill program into a payroll dispute.

Run it through payroll

For employees, a referral bonus is pay. In the US it is generally treated as supplemental wages and goes through payroll with tax withholding like any other bonus; the IRS covers supplemental wage withholding in Publication 15. Do not pay it with a gift card or a transfer from the founder's account to make it simpler. If you pay a referral fee to someone outside the company, such as an advisor or a friend, ask your accountant how to report it.

This is not tax or legal advice. Confirm the treatment with your payroll provider or accountant, especially if your team is in more than one country.

The things that matter more than the money

In the small companies where I have watched referral programs work, the bonus was the least interesting part. Three other things made the difference.

Make asking easy. Give the team a two-paragraph description of the role they can forward, and a short list of what you are really looking for. An engineer will not write your pitch for you.

Close the loop fast. If someone refers a friend and hears nothing for three weeks, they will not refer again. Every referral gets a reply within a few days, and the referrer hears the outcome, even if it is a no.

Respect the relationship. A referred candidate who is rejected badly is a problem for your engineer, not just for you. Treat referred finalists with extra care. I wrote about how to turn down a finalist without burning the relationship, and it matters double when a team member's friend is on the other end of the call.

One more caution. Referral-heavy teams tend to look like each other, because people know people from the same schools, companies and backgrounds. That is not a reason to skip referrals, but it is a reason to keep at least one other sourcing channel open, and to check that the team hiring from its own network is not quietly narrowing who you consider. I touched on the related question of a lead engineer hiring former colleagues.

When a referral bonus is the wrong tool

If nobody on your team is referring anyone, raising the bonus rarely fixes it. The usual reasons are that the team is overloaded, the role description is unclear, or someone is unhappy. Ask before you spend. A quiet "would you recommend this job to a friend? why not?" in a one-on-one will tell you more than any program design.

If you need a hire faster than your network can produce one, a recruiter or a fractional CTO running the search might be the right call for this round. I compare those options in using a recruiter for your first engineer. If you want a hand setting up the search itself, book a call.

FAQ

How much should a startup pay for an engineering referral?

There is no reliable benchmark. Vendor surveys quote anything from about $1,000 to $10,000. A single amount in the low thousands, paid in two parts, works well for most seed-stage teams and is far below typical recruiter fees.

Are referral bonuses taxable for employees?

In the US, generally yes. They are usually treated as supplemental wages and should go through payroll with withholding. Check with your payroll provider or accountant for your situation.

Should the bonus be paid when the new hire starts?

Splitting it works better: half at the start and half after 90 days or six months. That rewards good referrals and removes the need for a clawback.

Can I pay a referral bonus to someone outside the company?

You can, but the reporting differs from employee pay. Ask your accountant how to handle it before you make the payment.
