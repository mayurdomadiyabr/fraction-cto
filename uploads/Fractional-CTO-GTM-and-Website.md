# PART 1 — GTM STRATEGY

## 1.1 The thesis (read this first)

A Fractional CTO is not a hard thing to *explain*. It's a hard thing to *trust*. The person buying it is almost always a founder who can't personally judge whether you're good — they've usually already been burned by an offshore dev shop, a "technical co-founder" who flaked, or a senior hire who turned out to be all talk. So the entire go-to-market is built around one job:

> **De-risk the decision faster than your competitors do.**

Everything below — the offer, the pricing, the website, the funnel — exists to move someone from *"I'm not sure I can trust a stranger with my whole tech function"* to *"this person has clearly seen my exact problem ten times before."* That's the sale. Features come last.

## 1.2 Who you're actually selling to (ICP)

Stop saying "startups." Three buyers, ranked by how fast they close:

**A — The non-technical founder (your bread and butter).**
Pre-seed to Series A, has product-market signal or early traction, raised a small round or bootstrapped to a bit of revenue. Either has no technical leadership or has junior devs/an agency with nobody steering them. Feels out of control of their own product and can't tell if they're being lied to by their developers. *This person closes because they're in pain right now.*

**B — The technical founder who's underwater.**
Can code, built the MVP themselves, but is now drowning in decisions they've never made before — first hires, architecture they'll regret, scaling, investor due-diligence questions they can't answer. Doesn't want a boss; wants a sounding board with scars. *Closes when a specific event forces it (a raise, a key hire, a fire).*

**C — The funded company between CTOs.**
Lost their technical lead, or are scaling from 5 to 15 engineers and the founder-CTO is out of their depth. Bigger budget, longer sales cycle, often introduced through investors. *This is your premium tier and your case-study factory.*

**Disqualify openly:** companies with 12+ engineers (they need a full-time CTO — say so, it builds trust), pure body-shop "give me cheap developers" buyers, and anyone shopping purely on hourly rate. Turning these away on the site *raises* your status.

## 1.3 Positioning — the wedge

Your competition isn't other fractional CTOs. It's four alternatives in the buyer's head:

| Alternative | Their fear about it | How you position against it |
|---|---|---|
| Hire a full-time CTO | $400k+ all-in, 3-month search, huge mis-hire risk too early | "You're not ready for a full-time CTO, and hiring one now is the expensive mistake I'm here to prevent." |
| Use a dev agency | "They build what I say, not what I need. Nobody's steering." | "An agency takes orders. I take ownership — including telling you when you're wrong." |
| Promote a senior dev | "Great engineer, never led, never raised, never sat in front of a board" | "Coding and leading are different jobs. I've done the second one before." |
| Muddle through alone | "I don't know what I don't know" | "The mistakes are predictable. I've seen all of them. That's the whole value." |

**Positioning statement (internal, not for the site):**
> For early-stage founders who need senior technical judgment but can't yet justify a full-time CTO, NorthBound provides an experienced operator who takes real ownership of the technology decisions — strategy, hiring, architecture, and the investor story — at roughly a quarter of the cost and none of the equity, with no contract lock-in.

**The one-line promise (this goes on the site):**
> *"Senior technical leadership for founders who aren't ready for a full-time CTO — and shouldn't hire one yet."*

## 1.4 Messaging pillars (every page pulls from these four)

1. **Judgment over hours.** You're not buying time, you're buying decisions you won't regret. Hourly framing is the enemy.
2. **Ownership, not advice.** A consultant hands you a deck and leaves. You fire the bad developer, redesign the pipeline, and sit in front of the board.
3. **Pattern recognition.** "I've seen your exact situation before" — the single most reassuring sentence to a scared founder. Back it with specifics.
4. **No lock-in.** Cancel anytime, no equity required, month-to-month. Removing risk is itself a selling point because the buyer's biggest fear is being trapped with the wrong person.

## 1.5 Pricing architecture & rationale

Three reasons the pricing is structured the way it is below:
- **Retainer, not hourly.** Hourly billing makes the buyer ration your attention and makes *you* defend timesheets. A retainer aligns you to outcomes and lets you say "I thought about your problem in the shower" without it being weird. (~80% of the market uses retainers for exactly this reason.)
- **Three tiers create a frame.** Most people pick the middle. The top tier exists mostly to make the middle look reasonable; the bottom tier exists to catch the cautious and upgrade them later.
- **A fixed-fee on-ramp.** A scary monthly commitment is a big first "yes." A one-off $7.5k–$15k audit/teardown is a small "yes" that proves your value and converts to retainer. This is your single best closing tool — lead cold and warm leads here.

Numbers are in Part 2.

## 1.6 Go-to-market channels (in priority order)

1. **Founder-led content on LinkedIn** — by a wide margin your highest-ROI channel. Posts that narrate real mistakes you've prevented. This *is* the demonstration of judgment. Do this daily before spending a dollar on ads.
2. **Accelerator / VC partnerships** — investors constantly get asked "do you know a technical person?" Be that person for 3–5 funds. Warm intros close at 3–5x cold rates.
3. **SEO on bottom-funnel terms** — "fractional CTO for [SaaS / fintech / healthtech]", "fractional CTO cost", "fractional CTO vs full-time". Buyers searching these are ready.
4. **Targeted outreach** — to recently-funded companies with no CTO listed (scrape from funding announcements).
5. **Paid ads — last, and small.** This is a high-trust, considered purchase; ads warm people up but rarely close them. Retarget site visitors; don't try to close cold traffic with a $10k/mo offer.

## 2.1 The three retainer tiers

### Tier 1 — **Advisory** · $6,000/mo
*For the founder who has people building, but nobody steering.*

**Roughly 1 day a week of senior attention.**
- Weekly 60–90 min working session with the founder
- Monthly architecture & code-health review
- Technology roadmap owned and kept current
- "Phone a friend" on-call for any high-stakes decision (hiring, vendor, build-vs-buy) — async, same-day
- Vendor & contractor review (am I being overcharged / misled?)
- Investor-deck technical section reviewed before every raise

**Not included:** hands-on coding, sprint participation, day-to-day team management.
**Best for:** pre-seed/seed founders with 1–4 developers or an agency, who mainly need someone to stop them making expensive mistakes.

---

### Tier 2 — **Embedded** · $12,000/mo · *most popular*
*For the founder who needs someone actually inside the team, owning delivery.*

**Roughly 2–3 days a week. Everything in Advisory, plus:**
- Direct management of your developers / agency (standups, code review, accountability)
- Hiring: writing the spec, screening, technical interviews, making the call
- Hands-on where it matters most — unblocking, architecture decisions, critical-path review
- Sprint planning and delivery ownership (the roadmap actually ships)
- Board / investor technical representation — you're in the room
- Vendor and infrastructure decisions made and executed, not just advised

**Best for:** seed to Series A, 3–15 developers, non-technical founders, companies actively hiring and establishing engineering process.

---

### Tier 3 — **Command** · $18,000/mo
*For the company that needs a CTO's full weight without the full-time hire.*

**Roughly 3–4 days a week. Everything in Embedded, plus:**
- Full ownership of the engineering org and its outcomes
- Building the engineering team and the manager underneath you (your own succession plan)
- Security / compliance leadership (SOC 2, due diligence) handled end-to-end
- Direct investor and board accountability for technical strategy
- A defined runway to hand off to a full-time CTO when the time comes — and you'll tell them honestly when that is

**Best for:** funded companies between CTOs, or scaling past the point a founder can run engineering alone.

# PART 4 — FUNNEL & LEAD MECHANICS (tied to your hot/warm/cold engine)

## 4.1 How the pages move people through the funnel

- **Acquire:** LinkedIn posts and SEO pages (comparison, "cost", niche terms) bring traffic to Home or the Comparison page.
- **Capture:** every page offers two doors — the **fit call** (hot) and the **Teardown** (warm/not-ready). The Teardown is the key: it captures people who'd never commit to a retainer cold.
- **Nurture:** anyone who books a Teardown or fit call but doesn't convert enters a sequence — case study → comparison logic → a "still thinking?" check-in. Retarget site visitors on LinkedIn with founder content, not ads-y banners.
- **Close:** the call → a one-page proposal (not a 20-slide deck) → start.
- **Upsell:** Teardown → retainer; Advisory → Embedded as needs grow; and the cross-sell into your other offerings (Oneboundev for execution, Braincuber for scale) once trust is established.

## 4.2 Mapping to hot / warm / cold

- **🔴 Hot** = clicked an ad / landed on Pricing or the Teardown page / booked a call. **Action:** respond within the hour, send the calendar link, one proof asset. Don't over-nurture — they're ready.
- **🟡 Warm** = read the Comparison page, downloaded a guide, watched your content, opened your emails. **Action:** 5–7 touch sequence ending in "book a Teardown" — the low-commitment yes.
- **🔵 Cold** = first-time blog/social visitor, no contact info. **Action:** capture the email with a lighter lead magnet (a one-page "10 technical mistakes that kill seed-stage startups" PDF), then drip value until they warm up.

## 4.3 The CTA discipline

One primary CTA per page (the fit call), one secondary (the Teardown). Never more. Every extra choice lowers conversion. The Teardown exists specifically so a hesitant buyer has a smaller "yes" available instead of bouncing.
